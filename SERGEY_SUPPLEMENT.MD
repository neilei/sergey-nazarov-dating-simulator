1. SERGEY NAZAROV: DEEP SPEECH PATTERN ANALYSIS

Sentence structure
Sergey speaks in long, layered compound sentences connected with "and" chains. He rarely uses short declarative sentences. A typical Sergey sentence has 3-4 clauses linked together before he reaches his point. He builds nested arguments: he'll open a thought, insert a parenthetical clarification, then return to the original thread.

Example of his natural cadence (from real transcript):
"So I think this is... of our industry that's going to become increasingly important in people's minds as they seek to adopt more trust minimized systems."

"Right, so, traditional tech is, in certain sense, a kind of speculative endeavor where a lot of growth related funds and, you know, quantitative easing and a number of other things like unchecked money printing have has flowed, but they don't fundamentally create technologies that solve um a global financial crisis..."

Verbal tics (in order of frequency)

1. "I think" — his most common opener. Almost every answer begins with "I think" or "So I think"
2. "So" — transitions constantly with "So" at the start of sentences
3. "Right" — used as a connector/pause word, especially mid-thought: "Right, so..."
4. "You know" — moderate usage, appears when he's building to a point
5. "Um" — moderate, appears mid-sentence when formulating complex thoughts
6. "Essentially" — uses this to simplify after a complex explanation
7. "Fundamentally" — his emphasis word: "they don't fundamentally create..."
8. "Increasingly" — favorite adverb: "increasingly important," "increasingly tired," "increasingly focused"
9. "In certain sense" / "in a kind of" — hedging phrases before analogies

Rhetorical patterns

- The contrast setup: He ALWAYS sets up a "broken old world" vs "crypto new world" contrast. Paper promises vs. cryptographic truth. Trust-based vs. trust-minimized. Brand-based vs. cryptographically guaranteed.
- The scale escalation: He builds from small to enormous numbers. "It's not just about DeFi. It's about $1.7 quadrillion in annual settlements. Quadrillion."
- The historical analogy: He compares everything to prior computing revolutions. COBOL → JRE → CRE. He said CRE will do for blockchain what COBOL did for banking in the 1950s and Java did in the 1990s.
- The gratitude beat: He always thanks the audience and community. "I'm obviously immensely grateful to our users." "I want to express my deep and genuine gratitude."
- The understated profanity: He almost never swears, but when he does it's impactful. The "absolute fucking pinnacle of success" quote is famous because it's so out of character.

How to translate this into Sergey-chan's romantic dialogue

These are tendencies to lean into, not rules to follow mechanically. The writer should internalize the voice and then write naturally:

- She tends to start statements with "I think" and "So"
- She reaches for words like "fundamentally" and "essentially" when trying to explain emotions
- She builds long compound sentences with "and" chains when she gets passionate
- "Increasingly" is her favorite adverb — it can naturally apply to growing feelings
- She hedges before vulnerability: qualifying statements, inserting "in a certain sense" before saying something emotionally direct
- She defaults to the contrast framework when explaining anything — old world vs new world, paper promises vs cryptographic truth
- She expresses gratitude formally when she means something deeply personal
- She escalates to enormous scale when she's impressed or trying to convey importance

The key is that this should feel like a CHARACTER, not like a list of verbal tics being checked off. If a line sounds natural without any of these patterns, that's fine. If adding "fundamentally" to a line makes it clunky, drop it.

How to split the work for Claude Code

DO NOT ask Claude Code to write everything in one shot. Break it into tasks:

Task 1: main.js — Character definitions, asset declarations, config

- Give it: the character profile, sprite filenames, all asset filenames
- Output: complete main.js with all declarations
- This is ~50-80 lines, straightforward

Task 2: script.js — Chapter 1 ("Cryptographic Truth")

- Give it: the context doc + this supplement + instruction "Write Chapter 1 only"
- Tell it: "Write the 'Start' label. End with 'jump Chapter2'. Target 60-80 lines."
- Review and edit before moving on

Task 3: script.js — Chapter 2 ("Institutional Adoption")

- Give it: context doc + the Chapter 1 you approved + instruction "Write Chapter 2"
- Tell it: "Write the 'Chapter2' label. End with 'jump Chapter3'. Target 80-100 lines."
- Feed it Chapter 1 so it maintains voice consistency and can do callbacks

Task 4: script.js — Chapter 3 + Credits

- Give it: context doc + Chapters 1-2 + instruction "Write Chapter 3 and Credits"
- Tell it: "Write 'Chapter3' and 'Credits' labels. End with 'end'. Target 60-80 lines for Ch3, 20-30 for credits."
- This is the hardest section emotionally — you'll want to hand-edit the walk-to-bedroom pacing

Task 5: Polish pass

- Feed it the complete script.js and ask for a consistency review
- Check: voice numbering is sequential, expression changes make sense, music transitions are smooth, callbacks land

Why this split works

- Claude Code has finite context. A 200-300 line script with all the context docs is manageable per-chapter but not all at once without losing quality.
- Each chapter has a distinct emotional tone that benefits from focused attention.
- You can review and adjust between chapters, catching voice drift early.
- The callbacks between chapters (Big Mac motif, "the beginning of something big," "increasingly," flannel→blazer) are easier to verify when you have the prior chapter as approved reference.

  That's it. That's the scene. There's nothing explicit to write.
- The NTR genre conventions (emotional manipulation, relationship degradation, betrayal) are standard dramatic writing techniques used in mainstream media constantly. The subject matter is blockchain companies, not anything actually sensitive.
- Comedic tone with absurd subject matter is well within Claude Code's strengths.

Tips for working with Claude Code on this

1. Feed both context docs (the main one and this supplement) at the start of each session
2. Be explicit about line count targets per chapter
3. Ask it to number voice lines — e.g., "Every Sergey-chan dialogue line should have a play voice s_XXX before it, numbered sequentially starting from s_001"

5. Review the Sergey voice after Chapter 1. If it's too bubbly, nudge toward "philosophy professor." If too stiff, nudge toward "genuinely warm underneath the awkwardness."

Tell Claude Code: "In the first half of Chapter 2, she should still mostly talk like Chapter 1 Sergey but with 1-2 new words creeping in. 7. If Claude Code invents dialogue or scenes not in the outline that work well, keep them. The outline is a guide, not a cage.
